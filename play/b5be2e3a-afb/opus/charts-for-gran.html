Hedgehog asleep in a teacup

Cross-stitch. 49 stitches wide × 43 stitches tall. On 11-count Aida (big holes, easy on the eyes) it comes out about 11.3 × 9.9 cm; use 3 strands of thread. On 14-count Aida it's about 8.9 × 7.8 cm; use 2 strands. Cut your fabric at least 8 cm bigger all round.

One square = one cross. Empty squares are left as bare fabric. The red arrows point to the middle of the design; start stitching there so it ends up centred. The thick lines are every 10 squares, to help you keep your place.

Handy reminders

  • Fold the fabric in half both ways to find the middle, and start there (where the red arrows meet).
  • Make all the top stitches of your crosses lean the same way. It looks much neater.
  • Do one colour at a time. Where a colour is spread out (like the white spots), finish your thread off rather than carrying it more than 3 or 4 squares across the back, or it will show through.
  • The outlines are ordinary full crosses, so there's no backstitch to do.

Snowflake mittens

Stranded colourwork, knitted in the round. Two colours of DK wool: about 50 g of navy (main colour) and 25 g of cream (contrast). 4 mm double-pointed needles or a short circular for magic loop, plus 3.5 mm needles for the cuff. Tension: 24 stitches to 10 cm over the pattern. Makes an adult medium mitten (20 cm around the hand). Make two.

Navy square = knit with navy.
Cream square (with a little ring) = knit with cream.

The chart is 24 stitches wide. Each round you work it twice (48 stitches).

Read every row from right to left, starting at the bottom (row 1) and working up. Every round is a knit round.

A gold line sits above rows 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 to help you keep your place.

Wherever one colour isn't used, it never goes more than 5 stitches along the back, so there are no long loops to catch.

Making the mittens

  1. Cuff. With 3.5 mm needles and navy, cast on 44 stitches. Join into a round without twisting. Work k2, p2 rib for 6 cm.
  2. Change to 4 mm needles. Next round: *knit 11, make 1*, 4 times (48 sts). Put a marker at the start of the round.
  3. Hand. Join in cream and work rows 1 to 31 of the chart, doing the 24 stitches twice each round.
  4. Thumb hole (on row 10). On this round, knit the thumb stitches with a piece of scrap yarn in a different colour, slip those stitches back onto the left needle, and knit them again in the chart pattern.
    • Right mitten: use stitches 25 to 32 (the first 8 stitches of the second repeat).
    • Left mitten: use stitches 41 to 48 (the last 8 stitches of the round).
  5. After row 31, break the cream and carry on in navy until the mitten reaches the tip of the little finger.
  6. Top. Put a second marker after stitch 24.
    Decrease round: *knit 1, slip-slip-knit, knit to 3 stitches before the marker, knit 2 together, knit 1*, twice (4 stitches gone).
    Do the decrease round every other round until 32 stitches are left, then every round until 12 are left. Break the yarn, thread it through the last 12 stitches and pull it snug.
  7. Thumb. Carefully pull out the scrap yarn and slip the 8 stitches below and the 8 above onto needles. With navy, knit round them, picking up 1 extra stitch at each corner to close the gap (18 sts). Knit plain rounds until the thumb reaches about halfway up your thumbnail. Then knit 2 together all round (9 sts), break the yarn, thread it through and pull it tight.
  8. Darn in the ends and give them a gentle steam to even out the stitches.

Keep the strands at the back loose. Spread the stitches on the right needle out to their full width before you change colour, and the fabric won't pucker.

Sunflower coaster

Crochet, worked in rounds, right side always facing you. DK cotton in brown and sunflower yellow (small amounts of each), 4 mm hook. It comes out about 11 cm across.
UK terms are used. American names are shown in grey in the key.
Magic ring (or ch 4 and join into a ring)
Chain (ch)
Slip stitch (sl st)
Half treble (htr)  US: half double crochet, hdc
Treble (tr)  US: double crochet, dc
Double treble (dtr)  US: treble, tr

The circled red numbers are the round numbers. Brown symbols are worked in brown yarn and gold symbols in yellow. Each round starts at the stack of chain ovals and ends with a slip stitch (the dot).

Sunflower coaster: written out round by round

With brown:

  1. Round 1: Make a magic ring. Chain 3 (this counts as your first treble), then 11 trebles into the ring. Pull the ring tight. Slip stitch into the top of the chain-3. (12 stitches)
  2. Round 2: Chain 3, 1 treble in the same place. Then 2 trebles in every stitch all the way round. Slip stitch into the top of the chain-3. (24 stitches)
  3. Round 3: Chain 3, 2 trebles in the next stitch. *1 treble in the next stitch, 2 trebles in the next stitch*; repeat from * to * all the way round. Slip stitch into the top of the chain-3. (36 stitches) Fasten off the brown.

With yellow:

  1. Round 4 (the petals): Join the yellow with a slip stitch into any stitch. *In the next stitch work 1 half treble, 1 treble, 1 double treble. Chain 1. In the following stitch work 1 double treble, 1 treble, 1 half treble. Slip stitch into the next stitch.* That's one petal. Repeat from * to * all the way round (12 petals). The last slip stitch goes into the same stitch as the join. Fasten off.

Darn in the ends on the back. If the petals curl, pin the coaster out flat, spray it with a little water and leave it to dry.

If the middle starts to cup like a bowl, your trebles are a bit tight, so go up half a hook size. If it ripples, go down half a size.
